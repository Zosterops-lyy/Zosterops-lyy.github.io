Yang-Yang Li李洋阳

Ph.D. Student in Physics · Stony Brook University

I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at Stony Brook University, where I work with Tzu-Chieh Wei. I joined Stony Brook in 2025 after receiving my B.S. in Physics from the University of Science and Technology of China. At USTC, I studied theoretical physics in the Department of Modern Physics, School of Physical Sciences.

My research is in condensed matter theory and quantum many-body physics. I am interested in quantum spin systems and fermionic systems, with a focus on tensor networks, symmetry, symmetric mass generation, and topological phases. My current doctoral work concerns spectral gaps in AKLT models. I also work on the three-state Potts model on the fuzzy sphere, and have broader interests in conformal field theory and Hopf algebras.

My undergraduate research included projects with Yi-Zhuang You and Juven Wang on fermionic C-R-T symmetries and symmetric mass generation, and with Song-Bo Zhang on altermagnetism and topological edge states. These projects involved both first-quantized Hamiltonian theory and lattice many-body models. At Stony Brook, my doctoral research focuses on quantum spin systems, combining analytical work with numerical calculations.

Research

My research concerns how local structure, symmetry, and geometry constrain the spectra and phases of quantum many-body systems. I approach these questions through analytical models and numerical calculations.

Current researchManuscript in preparation

Spectral gaps in two-dimensional AKLT models

I study how the spectral gap of a two-dimensional AKLT model depends on its Bell-bond configuration and the spin-1 sites inserted along its bonds. The central question is whether local properties can establish a positive gap uniformly over different system sizes and configurations, even when the bond pattern is not translation invariant. This requires relating the geometry of local ground spaces to the spectrum of the full Hamiltonian.

A further challenge is the large number of local configurations introduced by random bonds and decorations. I investigate which configurations can be related by symmetry, and how a finite set of ground-space overlap estimates can control the gap criteria for the remaining cases. Tensor-network descriptions provide a way to represent and compare these local spaces.

With Tzu-Chieh Wei, Nicholas Pomata, and Wenhan Guo

Fermionic symmetries and mass generation

I am interested in how symmetry constrains fermion masses and the possibility of a symmetry-preserving gapped phase. The combined action of C-R-T and internal symmetries can forbid bilinear mass terms, while interactions may allow a gap. I study how these constraints depend on spacetime dimension and fermion content, and how they are related between continuum descriptions and microscopic lattice models.

Symmetry fractionalization · Anomalies · Mass generation

Topology and edge states

I study conditions under which boundary-localized states remain spectrally separated from the bulk across the full Brillouin zone. My work uses the BHZ model with altermagnetic and Zeeman fields to examine how the topology of the bulk bands relates to the existence and dispersion of these edge states, and which properties survive changes to the Hamiltonian.

Bulk topology · Floating edge bands

Critical phenomena on the fuzzy sphere

My current work on the three-state Potts model uses the fuzzy sphere to investigate critical behavior through finite-size spectra and operator structure. I examine whether these data are consistent with a three-dimensional conformal field theory and how finite-size effects influence that interpretation. Exact diagonalization provides a way to compare energy levels across accessible system sizes.

Finite-size spectra · Operator structure · Conformal field theory

CRT fractionalization in first-quantized Hamiltonian theory

Yang-Yang Li, Zheyan Wan, Juven Wang, Shing-Tung Yau, and Yi-Zhuang You

Physical Review B 113, 195102 (2026)

We establish an eightfold periodicity of the combined C-R-T and internal symmetry groups for Majorana and Dirac fermions across spacetime dimensions. For Dirac fermions, this differs from the twofold periodicity of the underlying complex Clifford algebra. The classification includes symplectic Majorana fermions in dimensions d + 1 = 5, 6, 7 (mod 8). We determine the symmetry action on fermion bilinear masses and mass manifolds, and show how domain-wall reduction connects symmetry structures in different dimensions.

Journal article

Floating edge bands in the Bernevig–Hughes–Zhang model with altermagnetism

Yang-Yang Li and Song-Bo Zhang

Physical Review B 111, 045106 (2025)

The altermagnetic BHZ model supports floating edge bands over broad parameter regimes. With an additional Zeeman field, an edge band can separate completely from the bulk states of both spin sectors throughout the Brillouin zone. We identify strong and weak topological regimes through Chern and winding numbers, respectively, and obtain explicit edge-state energies and wave functions. Within the floating-band regime studied, the localization length is essentially independent of momentum along the edge.

Preprint · revised 2025

Quantum many-body lattice C-R-T symmetry: Fractionalization, anomaly, and symmetric mass generation

Yang-Yang Li, Juven Wang, and Yi-Zhuang You

arXiv:2412.19691 (2024)

The C-R-T and internal symmetries of lattice fermions have flavor-dependent anomalies that constrain symmetric mass generation. We show that eight copies of staggered Majorana fermions or four copies of staggered Dirac fermions admit symmetry-preserving four-fermion interactions with a unique gapped ground state. The flavor constraints agree with results from Kähler–Dirac fermions and cobordism classifications. We also establish relations between lattice and continuum symmetry operators, identifying how microscopic lattice symmetries give rise to their low-energy counterparts.

Research experience

Present

Uniform spectral gaps in two-dimensional AKLT models

I lead the analytical and numerical work on two-dimensional AKLT models with random Bell bonds and spin-1 decorations, advised by Tzu-Chieh Wei and working with Nicholas Pomata and Wenhan Guo. I combine local gap criteria with tensor-network ground-space maps and classify bond configurations using Z₂ × Z₂ loop invariants, Clifford rotations, and spatial symmetries. This yields positive gap bounds independent of system size and bond configuration on the honeycomb, square-octagon, star, and cross lattices. For random decorations, I develop projective-metric covering bounds; on the cross lattice, these reduce 16,791,552 symmetry-reduced cases to 686 representative overlap calculations.

July–December 2024

C-R-T fractionalization in first-quantized Hamiltonian theory

Under the supervision of Yi-Zhuang You and Juven Wang, and in collaboration with Zheyan Wan and Shing-Tung Yau, I formulated Majorana fermions as Clifford-algebra representations and worked out the combined C-R-T and internal symmetry groups for Majorana and Dirac fermions. I calculated how the symmetries act on allowed fermion bilinear masses and mass manifolds, and used domain-wall reduction to compare the symmetry groups in different dimensions.

July–December 2024

Lattice symmetries and symmetric mass generation

Working with Yi-Zhuang You and Juven Wang as supervisors, I derived symmetry groups for lattice Majorana and Dirac fermions and analyzed ultraviolet–infrared symmetry matching, including emergent and emanant symmetries. I carried out the classification of symmetric mass generation in 0 + 1 through 3 + 1 dimensions and extended the analysis to higher dimensions. I used anomaly constraints and Mathematica searches to construct candidate symmetry-preserving gapping interactions, then checked their action numerically using Jordan–Wigner transformations and analytically using stabilizer methods.

June–August 2024

Floating edge bands with altermagnetism

Under Song-Bo Zhang's supervision, I carried out analytical and numerical calculations for the altermagnetic BHZ model. I derived phase diagrams using Chern and winding numbers, obtained nonperturbative expressions for edge-state spectra and wave functions, and calculated spin-resolved surface densities of states with iterative Green-function methods. I also tested the response of the edge states to geometric rotation, spectral asymmetry, and spin coupling, using these calculations to examine their stability.
